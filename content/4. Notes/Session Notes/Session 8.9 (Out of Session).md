# A Kalaman Interlude

## Scene 1: Meulara's Oddities

The bell over the door doesn't so much ring as *shriek with joy*, which is about right for the shop it announces.

After the hush of Wyhan's, with its polished counters and the sense of being quietly appraised and found wanting, Meulara's Oddities hits like a wave. Shelves lean in from every wall, stacked three deep with things that defy categorization: a taxidermied badger wearing spectacles, a jar of buttons that hum faintly when the moon is full, a birdcage with no bird and, according to the little tag, no *need* of one. In the middle of it all, arms already flung wide before the door has finished opening, stands Meulara: silver hair going in six directions, grin going in none but up.

"A CUSTOMER," she announces, to the shop, to the street, to whoever else might be listening. "Oh, I *knew* today would be good, I felt it in my knees. What are we hunting for?"

Asher explains: something for close work, something quiet, something that doesn't cost what he doesn't have. Meulara's eyes go bright, the look of a woman who already knows exactly which shelf to raid.

She vanishes behind a rack and returns wielding a dagger like a conductor's baton. "Found this in a lot from an estate sale. Watch." She draws it. Nothing. No whisper of leather, no ring of steel, just a blade that is suddenly out, as if it had always been out. "Spooky\! I love it. I've been waiting for someone with quiet eyes to walk in, and here you are."

She calls it **Whisper**. She asks two hundred gold for it, purely as a formality, then talks herself down before Asher can even open his mouth to haggle: "No, no, seventy-five's fair, don't argue with me, arguing with me is *my* job." She settles, delighted, for a trade and the promise of a good story told well. "The kind I can re-tell," she clarifies. "I have a reputation to maintain."

While she wraps it, she presses something small and scaled into his other hand, unprompted. "A river-trout scale. Vogler river, probably. Fisherman swore it was lucky, then sold it to me, so make of that what you will. Two steel and it's yours, and honestly I'd have just given it to you." A flicker crosses her face at the word *Vogler*, gone as fast as it came, a small kindness aimed at a wound she doesn't ask about.

She also shows off a hand crossbow some bored gnome traded her for a hat, "loads itself, mostly, it's only bitten me twice," and sets it aside on the counter with a knowing look, unpushy. Begrudgingly Asher also asks for the crossbow bringing his total up to 250 gp spent but he wasn’t about to walk out without a self loading crossbow… 

Asher leaves lighter in the coin purse (barely) and heavier in every way that matters, with a blade that makes no sound, a crossbow that solves the annoying need to reload mid combat, and a scale that might turn his luck.

## Scene 2: The Marshal's Ledger

Vendri's office smells like paper and cold tea. By the time the party finishes laying it all out across her desk, the ledger, the formula notes, the military scroll, the map, the seal, the letter, and the name *Lord Soth* spoken aloud in a room for the first time, she has stopped pretending to drink the tea at all.

She reads in silence. That's the thing about Vendri: she doesn't interrupt, doesn't gasp, doesn't perform her alarm for the room. She just goes very still, the way a soldier goes still before they move, and when she finally looks up her face has settled into the flat, focused calm of someone recalculating everything she thought she knew about the next month of her life.

"You did this in days," she says, and it doesn't sound like a question so much as a number she's filing away.

She turns the seal over in her fingers longest of all, and when she finally sets it down, she doesn't reach for it again.

"This is bigger than a smuggling ring. This is bigger than Vogler." She looks at the map next, at the marks on it, the routes, and her jaw sets. "I have soldiers. I do not have *eyes*. Not the kind this needs." A pause, deliberate, like she's tasting the words before she commits to them.

“There is someone that does, is willing to help, and who we’ve already relied on heavily to complete this mission. It’s time to squash the past and welcome nightvane into your fold once again. Bury the past Vendri, we need her.” \- Davran 

She doesn't love hearing it. That much is obvious. But Vendri didn't get her post by letting pride outrank the city.

"Go to her," she says. "Tell her what you told me, or as much of it as buys her cooperation without buying her leverage. If she'll work with me, even for a season, even through gritted teeth, I'll take it. Kalaman doesn't have the luxury of two factions doing this alone." She hands the letter back, but keeps the seal and the scroll, locked in the one drawer she locks anything in. "Bring me back an answer. Any answer. I need to know what I'm building a defense around."

## Scene 3: The compass rose

Nightvane's parlor is candlelight and quiet, and the quiet, in her domain, is never accidental.

She receives the party the way she receives everyone, perfectly warm and perfectly unreadable, until someone mentions Greyfeather, and something genuine flickers across her face before she can smooth it back into composure.

"He's resting," she says. "Eating better than he has in weeks, from what my people tell me. You want to thank me for that." A small, knowing smile. "You're welcome. And also, I don't do anything for free, so I appreciate that you've clearly come prepared to prove you understand that."

They lay the offering in front of her: not coin, but knowledge. Tinker's Row, curated with Vendri's report into exactly the type of information a woman like Nightvane would find worth having, capped by the piece that matters most. Oflyn. Dead, unmasked, and not what he appeared to be at all.

"A Sivak draconian," Davran explains, "wearing a dead man's face. They do that when they're killed in their true form, take on the shape of whoever slew them, for a day, maybe less. If you saw 'Oflyn' walking around after we fought him, it wasn't him. It was whoever he'd have wanted you to trust."

Nightvane goes still the way Vendri did, though on her it reads less like control and more like a held breath. This is the kind of thing that rewrites conversations she's already had and decisions she's already made, all of it built on a face she now knows was a lie waiting to happen.

"That," she says slowly, "is worth considerably more than I was expecting from a handful of adventurers three days off the road." She turns the information over, already recalculating who in this city might not be who they claim. Then, softer: "Thank you. Truly. For Greyfeather, and for this."

The opening is there, and the party takes it. Vendri, needs you. You both compliment each other where the other is weak. We’re going to need you both if this city is to remain standing. 

Nightvane's mouth does something complicated.

"The Marshal and I have never agreed on very much," she says. "But I find I agree with her that a burning city makes for excellent cover, and I don't like being caught unprepared." She pauses. "Tell her I'll hear her out. That's not a yes but rather a door unlocked."

## Scene 4: Two Powers, One City

The message goes back to both of them the same way: discretion is negotiable with allies. What isn't negotiable is what happens if you sit this out and lose.

It isn't subtle, and it isn't meant to be. Vendri has soldiers and no eyes. Nightvane has eyes and no army. Both of them know it, and both of them have known it for longer than either would admit. What they've been missing isn't the logic. It's a reason to move first without looking like the one who blinked.

The party gives them that reason, mostly by refusing to pick a side. They sit between the two factions and make it clear that whatever gets decided here is going to involve both of them, or neither, and Kalaman doesn't have time for neither.

Vendri comes to Nightvane's parlor. That alone is a concession, and everyone in the room knows it. Nightvane, for her part, has cleared out the candlelight theatrics. This meeting gets lamps, plain and bright.

There's a long moment where it could still go wrong. Old grudges have their own gravity. But then Vendri sets the real map on the table, the one with the marks the party brought back, and Nightvane leans over it, and the history between them falls away, replaced by two extremely competent women looking at the same problem from two different angles and figuring out, together, exactly how to solve it.

"Temporary," Vendri says, like she's daring the word to be a lie.

"Temporary," Nightvane agrees, in the tone of someone who has already decided it won't be.

They shake on it, brief and businesslike, and somehow more binding than any oath. Behind them, the party watches two of the most powerful women in Kalaman decide, in the space of a handshake, to run this city together. There's no fanfare to it. Just two former rivals talking over each other about patrol routes and dead drops, building something neither of them could have built alone, because a handful of newcomers refused to let them settle for anything less.

## Scene 5: The Empty Desk

Wyhan's shop doesn't invite visitors so much as permit them, and the party finds a way in after the main door is locked, knowing exactly how far out of their depth they might be.

They slowly tip toe not knowing what sort of trap, ambush, or worse might lie in weight from floorboard to floorboard. But, after sneaking, sliding, and moving with all their strength to make no noise or be noticed in any way… they find… nothing but the stillness of her shop. That is until Asher notices a note on Wyhan's desk

 Her desk is immaculate as always, except for a single folded note sitting square in its center, sealed in wax that hasn't fully cooled.

She's gone. No note beyond what's on the desk, no warning, nothing but the unmistakable sense that a very careful woman just did something very fast, and that whatever she knows was enough to move her before a conversation could even be started.

*To whoever finds this first.*

*I don't nromally leave notes so consider yourself special. I want that understood before you read the rest of this one.*

*What I’ve seen and gathered changes the very shape of the problem. I’ve found what I was looking for after stalling and buying time. What I’ve discovered could be the very thing that will hold the balance of all of Ansalon from falling to these armies and plummet into darkness.* 

*The High Mage Society needs to hear this from me, in person to believe and act on this discovery. I've gone to see to that.* 

*P.S. Whatever is wearing Lord Soth's name, or his shape, or something worse, is not finished with this city. I would advise extreme caution even if there is none to be had.*

*Don't come looking for me. I'll find you when the time is right.*

*— W.*

## Scene 6: A mission for a pigeon

The ring of sending sits strange on Greyfeather's hand at first, too fine a thing for someone who's spent years making himself small and unseen. He turns it over twice before he puts it on, and when he finally speaks the words that connect it, his voice comes through steadier than any of them expected from a man they left shaking days ago.

**First report**, a day later, comes clipped and careful: "Scouting party. Six of them, draconian-marked. Passed within thirty feet of me and never looked up. I don't know if that's my training or their carelessness. Going to assume the former until it kills me."

**Second report** comes stranger, quieter, like he's not sure he wants to say it out loud: "Saw a figure at the edge of a burned village. Tall. Wrong kind of tall, like the shape of a man stretched by something that isn't a man. Armor black as a wet river-stone, and cold. I felt it from a hundred yards. Didn't approach. Didn't need to. Whoever that is, I don't think 'careful' begins to cover what I should be around him." 

**Third report** a holding camp, small, poorly guarded but watched, built to notice a rescue coming from any obvious direction. Greyfeather doesn't wait for instructions. "Going in. If this goes wrong, tell them I said the city was worth it."

Then six hours of silence, the longest six hours any ring of sending has ever produced, before his voice comes back breathless and half-laughing with adrenaline: "Got her. She's furious, she's loud, and she's already complaining about the walk back, and honestly it's the best sound I've heard in a week. We're coming home."

Whatever fear Greyfeather carried into that camp, he brings something different out of it: the steadiness of a man who's decided, somewhere in the dark, exactly what he's willing to bleed for. With Oflyn dead and Wyhan gone, he no longer works for the party out of debt. He works with them because he wants to.

## Scene 7: The Gnome Under Siege

Twelve miles south of Kalaman, the Dargaard Mountains sit under their permanent shroud of cloud, and the party crests a rise to find a hillside that should be quiet and very much is not. Exactly what Vendri warned them of. Their task was simple, find and recruit the “local” Gnomish inventor who Nightvane found to be the engineer behind the dracolich machine the red dragon army used in it’s siege against Vogler. The gnome is presumed to not know exactly what she did but her mind and her inventions will be invaluable in the days to come. But alas none of that matters if what’s happening ahead takes her out\! The party rushes in to assist the Gnome.

The structure ahead looks like a cottage that lost an argument with a foundry: pipes hissing steam, gears turning for no reason anyone sane would need, and all of it currently under siege. Ten goblins in black armor stamped with Kansaldi’s spiral hurl themselves at a yard full of clockwork traps that spin, snap, and occasionally throw the goblins right back. A hobgoblin stands well clear of the chaos, barking orders the goblins are only loosely following, because the yard itself seems to have opinions.

Above the door, a clockface swings open like an eyelid, and a gnome leans out to hurl something small, brass, and extremely displeased with everyone in the vicinity. "NOT TODAY," she screams, clearly delighted with whatever she just built and threw.

The fight that follows is chaos stacked on chaos: goblins, traps, and a gnome heckling everyone including her own inventions. It ends the way these things tend to when the party's involved. Goblins down, hobgoblin fled or fallen, yard considerably more scorched than when they arrived.

The clockface swings open properly this time. Tatina Rookledust climbs down through it like she's done it a thousand times, because she has, already talking before her boots hit the ground. "Are you Dragon Army? You don't look Dragon Army. You look like people who just saved my workshop, which is a much better thing to look like."

Once she's satisfied they're not a second wave, the story spills out of her in one long breath. A stranger in red-and-black armor, weeks ago, bought her weed-clearing device with a request to make it look draconic, a request she now understands with sick clarity produced the very machine that tore through Vogler. A second commission, meant for pickup today. Instead, goblins showed up demanding "the weapons," and Rookledust, however impractical and outmatched, refused to hand over another one of her creations to be twisted into something cruel.

She doesn't wallow in it. "Can't fix yesterday," she says, already moving toward her workshop. "Can fix today. Give me one minute." She returns with a pair of small devices, warm to the touch, humming faintly. "Fargabs. Talk into one, the other hears it, doesn't matter the distance. I built them by accident trying to make a weapon. Funny how that goes. Sometimes the accident's better than the plan."

She hands the party one and keeps its twin. By the time they set off for Kalaman, she's already walking alongside them, still talking, sketching her next idea in the air with both hands.

## Scene 8: The War Room

The war room in Kalaman has never held this much competence in one place at one time, and it shows in the air itself: maps pinned edge to edge, markers moving as fast as the two women moving them.

Vendri and Nightvane work like they've been doing this for years instead of days, one voice picking up exactly where the other's trails off. Troop counts, dead drops, sightlines, contacts. Kalaman's soldiers and Kalaman's shadows are finally reading from the same page, and for once the party gets to watch their own work pay off in real time.

The group walks in with Rookledust in hand. Who simply sits in astonishment of the two much taller women of power atop a small stool she slides up inbetween them. Head darting from one woman to the other making her head spin. Too many words not enough numbers. She starts drawing and calculating beneath the two women on an empty space.

Shortly after the doors BANG open.

"IS THERE ROOM," bellows a voice that could strip paint, "for ANOTHER HAMMER?"

Cudgel fills the doorway like she was built specifically to fill doorways: armor dented in six new places since anyone last saw her, hair braided with what looks suspiciously like a goblin's finger bone, and a grin wide enough to swallow the room whole. She doesn't wait for an answer. She marches straight to the map table, plants both fists on it hard enough to rattle the markers, and looks between Vendri and Nightvane with open, delighted approval. Then glances at the small gnome with a smile directed towards a child.

"Well, DAMN," she says. "Look at you two. Finally working together."  
   
Before anyone can answer, the doors go again.

Darrett fills the second entrance the way Cudgel filled the first, except where she arrived loud, he arrives like weather: grizzled, blood marked, carrying ten new pounds of muscle, fatigue, and a stillness that wasn't there before. Whatever he's been doing since they last saw him has put a harder edge of certainty on his shoulders. He doesn't smile. He doesn't need to. The room straightens up a little just from him walking into it.

"Wheelwatch Outpost's gone," he says, no preamble, because Darrett has never once believed in easing into bad news. "Dragon Army took it. Twenty-four miles southeast, supplies our whole border patrol." His eyes go to the party. "Raven used to play in the fort in her youth when she visited her dad, I’ve asked her to come to give precise intel on the inner and outer workings of the fort. We need to retake it and fast."

Mayor Raven arrives last, because the reunion isn't finished until every piece is on the board, calm in the way of someone who's spent her whole career being the last calm thing in a room full of chaos. She lays it out clean and fast: the watchtower with its commanding view, the alarm horns on all four corners, the unknown size of the garrison, and the eight-hour march to a grove a mile out where troops can gather unseen.

Vendri, arms crossed, watches the map like it owes her something. "My preference is you get inside and open a gate before anyone sounds those horns. No dead soldiers, no burned fort. Beyond that, I'll take whatever plan gets it done."

For a moment, nobody says anything. The silence has a different weight than any before it: Vendri, Nightvane, Cudgel, Darrett, and Raven, standing in one room together, looking at the same map for the first time since this all began, with the party in the middle of it, the reason any of these people are speaking to each other at all.

It feels, for exactly one held breath, like the beginning of something the Dragon Army is going to regret.
