*Gather close now, and I will tell you a tale. Not of the great kings or the old gods, but of four souls from a river town called Vogler — a place so small you could miss it on a map, and so full of heart you'd never forget it once you'd been. This is where it began, as all the best stories do: with a funeral, a festival, and the end of everything they knew.*

---

They came together to bury a storyteller.

Ispin Greenshield — adventurer, spinner of legends, beloved liar — had finally told his last tale. He left behind no army and no fortune. Just a round green shield, painted mossy and carved with a broad-leaf tree, said to have been given to him by a unicorn in the depths of the Drakkenwood. And he left behind four young souls who had grown up on his stories and were about to stumble into one of their own.

There was **Gregor Fairmount**, a squire of twenty-six years, son of a one-handed Solamnic knight. Strong as a drawbridge and twice as earnest. The kind of man who quotes the Oath and the Measure before breakfast and means every word of it. There was **Asher Owyn**, the merchant lord's quiet third son, raised more by his mother's cleverness than his father's expectations — a young man who had learned to be the person everyone looked past, right until the moment they shouldn't. There was **Davran Raes'Vir**, an elf who had outlived most of his human friends and made new ones anyway, a tinker and toymaker the children called Mr. Coppertop. And there was **Dimitri Forgehammer**, a dwarf of few words and deep silences, who had once taken up a clericship and put it down again for reasons no one dared ask.

At Ispin's reception in the Brass Crab — that old tavern with aquarium tanks lining every wall and one great empty tank reserved for Benebog, the river monster whose very sighting was said to bring luck — Becklin Uth Viharin, a knight of Solamnia, presented them with Ispin's shield and a request: would they take part in the Battle of High Hill reenactment, the mock battle Ispin had always loved acting in? He played a dying soldier every year, and he played it with such gusto that the crowd wept and laughed in equal measure.

They said yes, of course. What else would you say to a dead man's favorite tradition?

But there had already been a warning. Days before the festival, a farmer's boy had gone missing near the edge of town. The four of them had ventured out and found him shaking in the tall grass. Beyond him, the aftermath of an ambush — Solamnic knights slaughtered on the road by creatures out of Ispin's oldest and darkest stories. Draconians. Spawned from corrupted dragon eggs, things that turned to stone when they died and breathed poison with their last gasp. The knights had been escorting a wagon to Vogler for the Kingfisher Festival, and something had not wanted them to arrive.

The four reported what they'd seen to Becklin, who told them plainly: do not act alone, get help if you need it, and above all, do not cause a panic during a time of celebration.

---

And so the Kingfisher Festival came to Vogler in all its color and noise. The town was alive — music and market stalls, games and laughter, the smell of bird pies and spiced drink on the wind. Dimitri destroyed the falconry competition so thoroughly they shut it down for the day. Gregor and Asher fumbled their archery but redeemed themselves in the rafting. And the Mayor herself — Mayor Raven — hooked Benebog from the river, the legendary catch, and the whole town helped reel the beast in. They put it on display in the Brass Crab's great tank, and for one perfect afternoon, Vogler was the luckiest place in the world.

Then came the reenactment.

The militia of Vogler lined up against Cudgel's Ironclad mercenaries in the village circle, play-acting the old victory of the Solamnic knights over Istar. The crowd cheered. Children sat on their fathers' shoulders. And then black-cloaked figures appeared on the cliffside above, and the sheen of real steel caught the light beneath the half-ogre's cloth.

The battlefield filled with blood and screams, and the crowd did not understand. They thought it was part of the show.

It was not part of the show.

---

What followed was chaos and courage in equal measure.

The mercenary Gragonis — a half-ogre who had been bought with dragon gold — led the betrayal. The four cut through the turncoats and put him down. Dimitri's blade was the one that ended it, swift and without ceremony, when Gregor asked for mercy and Davran hinted they might learn more from a living prisoner. The dwarf had his own counsel on such matters.

A dying mercenary gasped out the truth: Gragonis had returned to camp with gold and a promise. Betray Vogler or miss out on the riches. But not all had turned. A man named Jeyev had found a way around the treachery, refusing to join the reenactment. He rode in from the north with loyal riders and worse news.

An army. A red dragon. Machines of fire. Thousands strong, and marching on Vogler.

---

At the emergency town meeting, Becklin stood before the people of Vogler and spoke the words no one wanted to hear: the War of the Lance was upon them again. A rider arrived at the gates bearing a letter from Kansaldi Fire-Eyes, the Voice of Takhisis herself. Submit. Lay down arms. All rations, resources, and people now belong to the Red Dragon Army. Refusal means death.

They refused.

The plan was desperate and simple. Evacuate the town down the Vingaard River — women and children in boats, the rest ferried across to walk toward Kalaman on foot. The Ironclad and the four would stay behind to buy time. Becklin asked for an emergency signal: the town bell, rung, would mean all is lost, evacuate immediately. A gnome named Thrann offered his experimental "gnome flinger" — a catapult of dubious engineering — and warned them to pull the black lever, not the red or blue. Especially not the blue.

Becklin left a gift in her quarters for young Darit, the orphan boy she had looked after. When he opened it the next morning, he found custom-fitted knightly armor. He put it on with trembling hands. He became a knight, for a night.

---

The party launched from Thornwall Keep aboard the gnome flinger — which misfired, naturally, but they dodged the debris and landed on their feet at the cliffside. They ambushed the Dragon Army scouts and killed most of them, but a warhorn sounded before they could silence the last. From below came the terrible grinding sound of metal on metal, and through the darkness rolled a machine of black iron burning with immense heat — a Boilerdrak, a siege engine of fire and fury.

Davran, in a moment of either brilliance or madness, pulled a forbidden lever on the gnome flinger and launched the device itself into the first wave of cavalry. It worked. For a moment.

Then the draconians spread their wings from the cliffside and descended on Vogler like falling ash.

The streets became a nightmare. Red draconians swooping from above. A blue-skinned elf appearing for a single breath to say *run*, then vanishing. The voice of a dragon somewhere in the smoke. The four fought through the streets, clearing a path for civilians to reach the docks, and at the waterfront they found the Boilerdrak waiting — crewed by five draconians and hungry for blood.

They held the line. A great ogre joined the enemy and was put to sleep by magic, then plugged into the Boilerdrak's own workings. The machine exploded. The blast critically wounded Gregor and young Darit both. They hauled Darit's unconscious body onto a boat. Gregor, bleeding and half-broken, turned back one last time to save just one more person.

Then they were on the water, and Vogler was burning behind them.

---

They looked back.

On the docks, figures emerged from the smoke, dragging Becklin between them in chains. A woman in black plate armor — Kansaldi Fire-Eyes herself — stepped forward. Her gaze found them across the water, and she held it as she executed Becklin where she knelt.

Then Cudgel. Bound and pushed into the river. A dwarf in armor, doomed to sink.

The fires of Vogler shrank behind them as the boats drifted south. The party sat in silence — some somber, some furious, some hollow. Gregor, bleeding into the boards, tried to find words of hope. They were small words, and the darkness was very large.

But Darit was alive. The refugees were alive. And somewhere ahead, torchlight flickered on the water, and the walls of Kalaman rose against the night sky.

---

*That is how Vogler fell, and how four souls who had buried a storyteller became a story of their own. But do not weep too long, friend — for the tale does not end at the water's edge. Kalaman waited for them, with all its politics and secrets and shadows, and in those shadows something far worse than a siege engine was already stirring.*

*But that, as old Ispin would say, is a tale for another night.*
