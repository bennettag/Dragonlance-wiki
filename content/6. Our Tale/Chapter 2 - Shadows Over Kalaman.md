*You thought the fire was the worst of it, didn't you? That once our heroes reached the great walls of Kalaman, the danger would be armies at the gate and dragons in the sky — honest threats you can see coming and raise a shield against. But the Dragon Queen is cleverer than that, friends. The most dangerous thing in Kalaman wasn't waiting outside the walls. It was already inside them, wearing a dead man's face.*

---

They arrived upon torchlit waters, a flotilla of the displaced — fisherfolk and farmers, orphans and old soldiers, the entire population of a town that no longer existed. The citizens of Kalaman stared from the docks, bewildered. This was not a scheduled arrival.

Marshal Nestra Vendri met them at the harbor. A towering woman in her mid-forties, commander of the city's military, she carried herself like someone who had already counted the cost of the days ahead and found the ledger wanting. The Vogler refugees were escorted to the Almhouse of Mishikal. The party was directed to the Hall of Lords.

If they expected urgency from the city council, they were disappointed.

Six guild heads governed Kalaman, and not one of them was ready to hear what the four had come to say. Calof Miat was happy to house and feed the refugees but balked at the word "army." Sigmund Pross, head of the Shipwright's Guild — a man called "The Barge" — eagerly proposed assembling a fighting force, though his eagerness smelled more of opportunity than courage. Renold Costwyn offered nothing but sarcasm. Oflyn Vinhollow, the dockmaster, recommended doubling the harbor watch and doing precisely nothing else. Only Alexandra Nightvane, head of the Cartographer's Guild, looked them in the eye and asked the question that mattered: *What do you suggest?*

And in the gallery, Uri Stonegrey — master of information, whose eyes never stopped moving — watched them leave with a lingering gaze that Dimitri felt in his bones. Something had been sent. A message, a signal, a warning. To whom and for what purpose, the dwarf could not say.

As the doors of the Hall closed behind them, Sigmund asked someone to send for Bakaris — the same Bakaris who had fled Vogler with his wife Vessa and their son, slipping off the refugee ships before anyone could ask where they were going.

---

Nestra did not trust the council to act. She trusted four strangers from a burned town instead, which told you everything you needed to know about the state of Kalaman's leadership.

She met them at the Steady Beacon, a tavern run by a man named Jesen Thold, whose fingers brushed Nestra's hand for just a moment when he set down her drink. The Marshal laid it out plainly: the city was not ready. Three of her last four patrols had been ambushed, and only the council and her staff had known the routes. Someone was feeding intelligence to the Dragon Army. She named her suspects — the courier Greyfeather, the Fire Fishers dockworkers under Oflyn, and the purchases coming out of Wyhan's Apothecary — and gave them their mission: find who is selling out this city. Identify them, do not kill them. Report only through Jesen.

She warned them about Alexandra Nightvane: neither friend nor foe.

They visited the apothecary next. Wyhan was a Black Robe — a mage of ambition and pragmatism, not evil by creed but unconcerned with the comfort of others. She had once tested young adepts for the orders of high sorcery, but had withdrawn after a candidate's trial went wrong. Something about a Nothic and a student. No one pressed for details.

The shop was warded with abjuration magic. Pigeons came and went from the roof. A burly dockworker with an anchor on one arm and a red fire-wave on the other collected a packet and left without a word. And when Davran stepped inside, a voice slid into his mind like a knife into still water:

*"Something in you is waking up, is it not? Or perhaps coming back?"*

And then, quieter: *"Your vision, the one you ran from. It wasn't a vision of something that might happen. It was a memory of what will come to pass."*

Wyhan gave them a healing potion and a potion of fire resistance, said only that she was "doing what she should be doing," and sent them on their way.

---

Alexandra Nightvane's estate was everything Vogler was not. Halls lined with maps — the collected work of generations of cartographers, her family's legacy inked across parchment and vellum. She had already marked Vogler's fall on her charts. Just a detail. Just a town-shaped absence where a town used to be.

Over a lavish dinner, her guard came down. She spoke of her mother, of her own days mapping the far reaches of the world, and the conversation turned from courtesy to conspiracy. She offered the party a deal: her network, her reach, her read on every power player in Kalaman — in exchange for information. Specifically, she wanted to know what Marshal Vendri was doing with the city's resources. She suspected the Marshal's reports to the council were editorialized, and Nightvane did not abide incomplete maps of any kind.

Her assessments of the council were surgical. Sigmund and Bakaris were conspiring — building something, and fast. Calof was clean but spineless. Uri was an enigma even she couldn't chart. Renold was too busy chasing women and fearing a family curse — his father and grandfather had both drowned, and he avoided water like a cat. And Oflyn — well, Oflyn had problems. Three of his dockworkers had filed complaints. All three had quit within a week. One left for the capital and was never heard from again.

Davran demonstrated the Dragon Army's sigils through illusion, and Nightvane studied them with the eye of a woman who had spent her life cataloguing the world's dangers. When they described the Boilerdrak, she recognized the design as old work from the south and drew the conclusion the party had been circling: this army did not raid towns. It conquered cities.

She gave them a city map with locations marked — guild halls, people in her network, places they could trust. Two marks had no descriptions. One near the docks. One near Wyhan's apothecary.

When Davran pressed her on her true motives, she answered simply: family. Lineage. The Nightvane name on every map in this city, and a desire to see it survive what was coming.

Gregor and Alexandra sparred over philosophy — she posed a trolley problem, he dismissed it as unrealistic — and for a moment, the table felt almost normal. Almost like dinner among allies, rather than a negotiation between desperate strangers.

They agreed to share information going forward.

---

That night, Asher and Dimitri crept to the docks while Gregor and Davran kept watch from a distance.

The building Nightvane had marked was on the fourth pier — lights burning late, a crane extended upward, the sound of water inside suggesting an interior dock hidden from view. Smoke curled from a window. A wheelbarrow outside matched one they'd seen at Wyhan's. And pigeons — always pigeons — roosting on the eaves.

Asher intercepted one of those pigeons and pulled a coded message from its leg. Then he pressed himself to the wall and listened. Two voices drifted through the cracks — one foreign, one local:

*"When the Highlord's column reaches the valley road, the gate schedule changes. Vinhollow knows what to do."*

He slipped inside and found a desk covered in papers. A note detailed the eastern patrol window — second bell to fourth, River Gate, three guards instead of four. "The offering stands. When the standard is raised we will be ready. V. trusts nothing. Keep the channels clean. Burn this." The pigeon's message read: "Masked rider to the north. Greyfeather's pigeon went missing. O is concerned, are we still set for the plan?"

Crate markings read "Tinkers Row Consignment" with a stylized C, alongside a reagent list.

The web was becoming visible now. Greyfeather. Oflyn. Wyhan. The docks. Tinker's Row. River Gate. All connected, all pointing toward a single terrible purpose: when the Dragon Army arrived, the gates of Kalaman would open from within.

---

They hit the dockhouse at dawn. Through the skylight, dropping in on the Fire Fishers mid-operation. Boilerdrak components were scattered across the floor — stamped with the red dragon insignia, crated for delivery to a warehouse on Tinker's Row. The workers protested ignorance, but under pressure they named Greyfeather and Oflyn as the men they answered to.

A cloaked figure outside caught their eye and bolted.

What followed was the kind of chase bards love and city guards hate. The figure vaulted rooftop to rooftop, loosing a pigeon southward that Davran struck from the sky before it could carry word. Asher dropped a fog cloud across the rooftops, but the figure's glasses flashed blue and it navigated the murk as if it weren't there. Gregor commandeered a horse — "borrowed" would be generous — and thundered through the streets below while Davran attempted a heroic leap from a rooftop onto the saddle behind him. He missed the landing badly, barely hauling himself up, but the horse kept running.

Asher struck the figure down. They caught up on horseback.

The cloaked figure was Greyfeather himself.

---

At the Steady Beacon, with Jesen watching from behind the bar, they pressed the courier for everything he had. He was a nervous man, the kind who survives by knowing which way the wind blows and running with it.

His route was simple: dead drop at the docks, then north to a masked rider where the Vingaard forks. He'd made the run six times now. The rider never spoke. The rider was unsettling in a way Greyfeather couldn't articulate, the kind of wrong that lives in the spine rather than the mind.

When they mentioned Nightvane, opportunity flickered across his face. When they mentioned Wyhan, he flinched. "She's dangerous."

In exchange for asylum with Nightvane, he talked.

They planned to sack the city. Oflyn was behind it. Wyhan was involved, though she might not know the full scope. Greyfeather had seen something drag Oflyn's body into a basement on Tinker's Row — and the next day, Oflyn was walking around as if nothing had happened. But the man who came back was not the man who went down those stairs. Something was wearing Oflyn's face.

He handed over a coded message: an internal force would help sack the city, armed with guard patrol paths and intelligence about Kalaman's defenses. Efforts had already undermined Nestra's authority and redirected her scouts. He insisted no other council members were involved.

Jesen protested — Nestra wouldn't approve of brokering asylum behind her back — but agreed to escort Greyfeather to Nightvane's estate. Before they left, Jesen mentioned, almost offhandedly, that someone Gregor would really like to meet had been staying at the Beacon. He offered nothing further.

---

They went to 39 Tinker's Row expecting a warehouse. They found a tomb.

In the basement, inside a chest, lay the real Oflyn Vinhollow. Dead. His body stiff and cold, killed months ago by something that had taken his shape and walked into the council chambers wearing his face.

Above them, voices. They pressed against the ceiling and listened.

"Oflyn" — the thing wearing his skin — was speaking with Wyhan. He pressed her: did she want the city sacked? He argued that being sacked was preferable to being razed. Wyhan's answer was bitter and honest — the city had turned its back on her, and she was willing to let it burn. Plans were already in motion, he told her. He had spoken of her to powerful allies. He told her to relay to Kansaldi that she would consider her offer. She would have the reagents ready when they were needed.

They discussed the trouble at the docks. They knew it was the party. But they were confident the four knew little — not even about Tinker's Row.

"Oflyn" promised Wyhan a place working alongside the Red Dragon Army. Then he said something that hung in the basement air like smoke: *"The red dragon army that looms beneath."*

Beneath what? Beneath the city? Beneath the surface of what they thought they knew?

Then the thing that was not Oflyn said it needed to check on the basement.

---

When it descended the stairs, they were waiting.

It shed Oflyn's face like a snake sheds skin. What stood before them was a sivak draconian — silver-scaled, tall, and terrible, wielding twin scimitars and a lashing tail. It was faster than anything they had fought before, harder to hit, and every blow it landed was devastating.

In the chaos, the sivak hurled one of its scimitars at an explosive bottle on the desk — aiming to detonate whatever Wyhan had been brewing. Gregor intercepted the throw, because that is what Gregor does. The bottle did not break. The room did not burn. Not today.

The fight was ugly and close. The creature bloodied all four of them, and for a few terrible heartbeats the outcome was uncertain. Then Asher found his opening — quick and quiet as his mother taught him — and drove a dagger into the sivak's neck. It fell.

On its body, a scroll bearing five words:

*"Lord Soth is ready."*

---

*And that, friends, is where the tale stands — unfinished, as the best ones always are at this hour. The false Oflyn is dead. The real one rots in a chest. Wyhan brews fire in her apothecary and tells herself she is the one using them. Nightvane charts her maps and watches everything. Nestra commands an army that may already be hollowed out from within. And somewhere beneath Kalaman, in catacombs older than the city itself, waits the tomb of a knight who fell so far that death itself could not hold him.*

*The Highlord's column marches south. The gate plan holds. And Lord Soth — the Knight of the Black Rose, the Fallen One, the Death Knight who chose pride over the salvation of the world — is ready.*

*Ready for what? Ah, that is the question that keeps old storytellers up at night, turning it over and over like a coin with no good side.*

*Come back tomorrow, and perhaps I'll have an answer for you. Or perhaps the answer will have found us first.*
